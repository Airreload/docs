## The problem: setup gets in the way

You want to see a change on your actual phone. Before you can use hot reload, you need to get a debug build onto it and connect your development tools. That can mean finding a USB cable, enabling developer options, authorizing ADB, or configuring wireless debugging.

That setup interrupts a simple loop: **change your code, see it on your phone, repeat**.

## Our approach: run, scan, reload

Airreload brings building, installation, and connection into one local workflow:

1. **Run `airreload run`** in your Flutter project. The CLI prepares the debug integration and opens a pairing QR.
2. **Scan with Airreload Go.** Your phone pairs with the computer, the CLI builds a matching APK, and Go downloads it. Approve Android's installation prompt and open your app.
3. **Keep building.** Airreload connects Flutter to the running app. Edit your code and press `r` to hot reload, or `R` to hot restart. DevTools is available on your computer.

No USB cable, ADB setup, or developer options are needed for this flow. Your computer and phone communicate over the same trusted local network.

## Your project, your workflow

Keep using your editor and your existing Flutter project. Airreload prepares its integration in a separate session copy, so your original source and FVM configuration stay in place. Supported Dart edits under `lib/` can hot reload; assets, dependencies, and native changes still need a rebuild.

Airreload is currently for **Flutter Android development**. It builds debug APKs, and Android stays in control of installation.
